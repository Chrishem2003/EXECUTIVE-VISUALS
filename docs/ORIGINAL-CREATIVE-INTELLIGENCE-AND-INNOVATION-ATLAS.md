# Original Creative Intelligence & Innovation Atlas

**Status:** product design system and phased build plan. The Creative DNA compiler is implemented in the AI Director. The other concepts below are proposals until their implementation and tests are recorded.

## Product principle

Do not imitate a collection of competitor screens. Build an original creative operating system around a single durable object: **creative intent**. Intent connects the audience promise, emotional outcome, evidence, visual grammar, sound identity, rights, production graph, edits, quality review and final deliverables.

The system should make creative work more coherent, explainable, repeatable and commercially useful—not merely provide more buttons.

## The combinatorial idea engine

A useful idea engine should generate candidates, not claim that every combination is good. It can compose and score ideas across independent design dimensions:

| Dimension | Example option families |
|---|---|
| Narrative engine | mystery, transformation, investigation, demonstration, debate, journey, reversal, parallel lives, countdown, origin, challenge, field report |
| Audience emotion | wonder, trust, urgency, belonging, delight, courage, calm, surprise, ambition, empathy |
| Visual grammar | macro-to-wide, locked tableau, motivated handheld, graphic match, spatial reveal, split perspective, time compression, silhouette, long take, kinetic typography |
| Camera language | push, pull, orbit, lateral track, crane, handheld drift, static precision, subject-led motion |
| Light and color | naturalistic, high-key, chiaroscuro, practical-only, monochrome, complementary, restrained earth palette, neon accent |
| Sound language | rhythmic Foley, motif, silence, environmental bed, spatial sound, voice-led, percussion, tonal texture |
| Structure | cold open, question loop, three-act, proof-first, reveal-first, braided story, modular chapters, reverse chronology |
| Interaction | choose-a-path, annotated pause, audience prediction, comparison reveal, QR follow-up, community response |
| Delivery | vertical short, square feed, widescreen hero, silent-first, caption-led, audio-first, presentation, interactive web |
| Constraints | one location, one performer, natural light, phone-only, fixed runtime, no-dialogue, low bandwidth, strict brand kit |
| Evidence mode | live demonstration, measurement, expert source, document, user testimony, before/after, transparent uncertainty |
| Commercial intent | awareness, education, qualified lead, conversion, retention, recruitment, fundraising, community participation |

Even a conservative subset of these dimensions yields thousands of possible creative directions. The product should prune invalid combinations, surface the reason a candidate is interesting, and let the director choose. It must not pretend combinatorial volume equals originality.

## Original product concepts

### 1. Creative intelligence and direction
1. **Creative DNA** — persistent creative promise, audience contract, emotional journey, visual grammar and sonic identity.
2. **Distinctiveness stress test** — identify generic phrases and ask what only this production can show.
3. **Audience contract checker** — every opening promise must be paid off before the end.
4. **Emotional pacing map** — visualize intended feeling by scene and compare it with edit duration.
5. **Narrative tension curve** — flag long stretches without new information, stakes or change.
6. **Hook laboratory** — generate different mechanisms (contradiction, consequence, curiosity, human stakes), not superficial rewrites.
7. **Proof-first director** — build the story around what can actually be demonstrated.
8. **Idea collision studio** — combine two unrelated concepts and test whether the result has a coherent audience promise.
9. **Creative constraints as style** — turn limited locations, cast, time or equipment into intentional creative rules.
10. **Audience comprehension rehearsal** — predict jargon, context gaps and cognitive overload for human review.

### 2. Cinematic language and visual authorship
11. **Visual grammar compiler** — translate mood words into framing, movement, lens, light, palette and composition guidance.
12. **Motif continuity graph** — track recurring shapes, colors, props, gestures and transitions across scenes.
13. **Shot necessity score** — ask what information, emotion or transition a shot contributes.
14. **Camera motivation checker** — flag movement that has no narrative or spatial reason.
15. **Visual rhythm map** — compare shot length, motion energy, scale changes and narrative intensity.
16. **Negative-space director** — design composition around text, product placement, subject gaze and future crops.
17. **Color story evolution** — track how palette changes with character or story state.
18. **Transition grammar** — define a small intentional vocabulary of match cuts, sound bridges and motivated transitions.
19. **Spatial geography lock** — preserve screen direction, eyelines and location layout.
20. **Frame signature search** — retrieve the project's strongest visual references by composition and role.

### 3. Character, product and world continuity
21. **Character state ledger** — track age presentation, wardrobe, injuries, props, mood and scene chronology.
22. **Product truth lock** — preserve package geometry, labels, colors, logos and proportions across shots.
23. **World rules engine** — store physical, historical and fictional rules that a scene must not violate.
24. **Continuity fingerprint** — compare approved references and candidate frames against a project-specific identity signature.
25. **Wardrobe and prop state transitions** — model what changes and when.
26. **Location topology** — map entrances, windows, light direction and camera positions.
27. **Performance continuity notes** — record gaze, gesture, energy and delivery across takes.
28. **Reference confidence labels** — distinguish approved canon, inspiration, uncertain and prohibited references.

### 4. Sound and performance
29. **Sonic identity system** — reusable motifs, Foley palettes, ambience and silence rules.
30. **Dialogue intelligibility guard** — detect speech masking and inconsistent loudness.
31. **Sound-to-cut suggestions** — align edits to meaningful sonic events, not arbitrary beats.
32. **Voice performance map** — annotate emphasis, pauses, pace and emotional intent.
33. **Localization performance parity** — preserve timing, intent and emotional emphasis across languages.
34. **Audio provenance ledger** — trace music, effects, voice source, license and edit history.
35. **Silence as an editorial tool** — find moments where removing sound increases impact.

### 5. Editorial and adaptive storytelling
36. **Intent-aware timeline** — connect every clip to the beat or promise it serves.
37. **Branching edit versions** — explore alternative openings or endings without overwriting the master.
38. **Semantic edit search** — find moments by spoken phrase, visual subject, action, emotion or timecode.
39. **Change-impact map** — show which scenes, captions, dubs and exports become stale after an edit.
40. **Audience-specific cut compiler** — create platform versions from story beats and safe zones, not blind crops.
41. **Pacing diagnostics** — detect dead air, repeated information and abrupt tonal shifts.
42. **Narrative-aware captions** — preserve emphasis, timing and reading speed.
43. **Review disagreement resolver** — group conflicting feedback and identify the decision owner.
44. **Edit explainability** — show why a cut, crop or suggested revision was proposed.
45. **Non-destructive revision branches** — compare, merge or discard edit experiments.

### 6. Evidence, trust and rights
46. **Claim-to-shot ledger** — map factual claims to sources or visible demonstrations.
47. **Synthetic media provenance card** — disclose where generated, altered or reconstructed media appears.
48. **Rights expiry radar** — alert before music, likeness, location or footage permission expires.
49. **Consent scope graph** — bind a person's approval to specific uses, channels and expiry dates.
50. **Brand truth validator** — compare output against approved claims, product facts and brand rules.
51. **Uncertainty labels** — distinguish verified facts, estimates, dramatization and creative interpretation.
52. **Misleading-edit review** — flag edits that materially change the meaning of a quote or demonstration.
53. **Export provenance manifest** — include asset hashes, parent lineage, model/version and edit snapshot.

### 7. Model and engine intelligence
54. **Capability-aware director** — plan only with input/output limits actually declared by the selected engine.
55. **Blind model jury** — compare anonymous results against a project-specific rubric before revealing provider identity.
56. **Model drift sentinel** — detect when a provider update changes output consistency.
57. **Prompt delta ledger** — record which prompt change caused a meaningful output change.
58. **Reference compatibility checker** — detect unsupported media roles, counts, dimensions or duration.
59. **Quality-per-cost frontier** — compare verified quality against measured time and cost, not marketing claims.
60. **Fallback rehearsal** — test an alternate engine with the same inputs before a production outage.
61. **Local-first routing** — prefer a verified local operation when it meets the requested quality and privacy constraints.
62. **Model deprecation migration** — identify affected workflows and suggest compatible replacements.

### 8. Collaboration and production operations
63. **Creative decision log** — preserve who chose a direction, why and which alternatives were rejected.
64. **Async director's room** — collect timecoded review, questions and approvals in one structured space.
65. **Role-aware workspace** — separate creator, director, reviewer, rights approver and publisher permissions.
66. **Production pulse** — show blockers, stale approvals, expiring assets and schedule risk.
67. **One-click handoff package** — deliver brief, shot plan, assets, timeline, captions, rights and provenance.
68. **Recovery rehearsal** — test whether a project can be restored from durable assets and event history.
69. **Budget envelope planner** — reserve spend by stage and prevent late-stage cost surprises.
70. **Production carbon/compute estimate** — record compute and render usage where reliable measurement exists.

### 9. Accessibility and inclusive delivery
71. **Accessibility-first storyboard** — plan readable text, contrast, sound cues and non-audio meaning before generation.
72. **Caption comprehension test** — inspect speed, line length, speaker labels and reading complexity.
73. **Audio description planning** — identify visual information that must be conveyed for non-visual access.
74. **Color perception preview** — review key visual distinctions under common color-vision conditions.
75. **Low-bandwidth delivery compiler** — generate sensible bitrate and file-size variants.
76. **Localization layout stress test** — test text expansion, subtitle density and right-to-left layout before export.

### 10. Commercial and audience learning
77. **Creative hypothesis board** — state what each variant is testing before publishing.
78. **Variant learning ledger** — connect performance metrics to creative changes without claiming causation from weak data.
79. **Creative fatigue monitor** — identify repeated hooks, compositions and claims across campaigns.
80. **Product proof library** — reuse verified demonstrations and approved claims across campaigns.
81. **Audience journey storyboard** — connect awareness, consideration, conversion and retention assets.
82. **Offer clarity check** — ensure the product, audience, benefit, proof and next action are unmistakable.
83. **Campaign asset completeness** — detect missing aspect ratios, captions, thumbnails and localization.
84. **Post-campaign learning loop** — turn measured results into new hypotheses, not automatic creative decisions.

## Original interaction and interface direction

- **Command surface:** a calm command centre that explains what is happening, what is blocked and what needs a human decision.
- **Intent-first workspace:** the brief and its creative DNA stay visible as context across director, storyboard, timeline, enhancement and campaign modules.
- **Three levels of detail:** Focus (make a decision), Studio (edit production details), and Technical (inspect parameters, provenance and diagnostics).
- **Canvas + timeline + story map:** linked views of the same production graph, not three disconnected documents.
- **Decision cards:** show proposal, reason, expected benefit, risk, cost/latency if known, confidence/evidence and approval requirement.
- **Visual compare:** synchronized before/after, reference/output, version A/B and crop-safe review.
- **Progressive disclosure:** keep advanced controls hidden until the user needs them; expose keyboard shortcuts and accessible alternatives.
- **No fake live states:** a planned feature, configured adapter, tested adapter and production-ready integration must look different.
- **Recovery-first autosave:** visible save state, revision history, conflict resolution and recoverable branches.
- **Human control:** preview before execution, clear cancel/undo boundaries and no silent publishing.

## Release discipline

Every idea must have an owner, user problem, product surface, data contract, capability declaration, implementation status, tests, security/privacy review and measurable acceptance criteria. Do not label an idea “built” because it appears in a catalogue. Do not call a provider, incur a charge, publish content or modify production infrastructure without the appropriate configuration and approval.
